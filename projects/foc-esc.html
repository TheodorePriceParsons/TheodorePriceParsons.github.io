How it works

Overview

A field-oriented control (FOC) ESC I designed from a blank KiCad schematic to a spinning motor. It drives a GM4108H gimbal motor from a 3S LiPo with closed-loop current and speed control, running on an STM32G474 (NUCLEO-G474RE).

Hardware

  • DRV8305 three-phase gate driver with AON7544 MOSFETs; 5 mΩ shunts so the board can also handle bigger motors.
  • MT6701 magnetic encoder in SSI mode with a 6 × 2.5 mm diametrically magnetized NdFeB magnet.
  • 4-layer stackup: L1 power stage and phase copper, L2 solid ground, L3 dedicated PVDD plane, L4 signals plus doubled phase copper.
  • XT60 battery input and 2 mm bullet connectors for swappable motor leads.
  • Half-bridge behaviour checked in LTspice before layout; assembled by JLCPCB.

Firmware

  • PWM from HRTIM1 (the G474's high-resolution timer) with ADC current sampling triggered from HRTIM events.
  • Clarke and Park transforms, min/max SVPWM in volts, and PI current loops with conditional-integration anti-windup, all written by me.
  • Speed loop layered on top of the current loop.

Rev 1 → Rev 2

  • Rev 1 shipped with real mistakes: MT6701 CSN/DO pins swapped, two 3.3 V nets that were never joined (leaving the DRV8305 VREG and pull-ups unpowered), no VBUS sensing, and a mounting pattern that didn't match the real motor. I designed a 3D-printed adapter plate in Onshape to bridge the holes.
  • Rev 2 added a proper 10k/3.3k VBUS divider and put the encoder and gate driver on one shared SPI bus with separate chip selects.

Debugging story

On rev 2 the DRV8305 kept asserting nFAULT. I narrowed it down with a GPIO test loop and a logic analyzer, and the culprit turned out to be a faulty bench power supply, not the board.
